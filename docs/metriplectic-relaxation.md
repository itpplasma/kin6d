# Metriplectic relaxation on the FEEC complex

Status: implemented on the NN1 real-Fourier complex
(`src/mhd/nonnested_relax.f90`), pointwise leaf and identities generated by
`gen/gen_metriplectic.f90`, oracles in `test/test_nonnested_relax.f90`.
Claims are labelled [V] (verified here by proof, test or source read) or [H]
(hypothesis). Decision 85 of the programme asks for this stage as the
globalisation in front of Newton and as the topology-preserving ideal option
for non-nested equilibria.

## Role

Newton on the equilibrium residual converges fast near a solution, but its
merit can stall far from one, and on non-nested problems it does not say which
branch (island phase, topology) it converges to. A relaxation that lowers the
magnetic energy while it keeps flux, helicity and optionally field-line
topology fixes the branch by its invariants and brings the iterate into the
Newton basin. The stage ends with an explicit hand-off test, after which the
existing Newton solver of the same discrete equation takes over.

## Continuous bracket

Take A as the state (B = curl A plus the harmonic toroidal field h = F/R e_phi),
the magnetic energy W = int |B|^2/(2 mu0) as the dissipated functional and the
helicity K = int A.B as the conserved one. A metric (symmetric, positive
semidefinite) bracket

    (F, G) = - int dF/dA . G[B] dG/dA

gives dA/dt = -G J with J = curl B = dW/dA (mu0 = 1 below) and

    dW/dt = -(J, G J) <= 0,    dK/dt = -2 (B, G J) = 0   if  G B = 0.

The Casimir condition G B = 0 is the whole design rule [V, identity below].
Two metrics are implemented.

* Ideal, pointwise: G = D(B) = |B|^2 I - B B^T. Then dA/dt = (J x B) x B, the
  magneto-frictional law E = -u x B with u = J x B (He, Farrell, Hu and Andrews,
  arXiv:2501.11654, eq. 4 [V, paper body]). Its fixed points are force-free
  fields. In the continuum the flow is an ideal (frozen-in) transport, so every
  field-line invariant is kept; this is the double-bracket picture of Flierl and
  Morrison, which conserves all Casimirs of the ideal Poisson bracket (cited in
  Bressan et al., arXiv:1809.03949, introduction [V]). D(B) is the
  collision-like kernel |g|^2 I - g g^T of Bressan et al. with g = B instead of
  the gradient of dH/du [V, their eq. for T_ij and D(x)].
* Taylor, global: G = I - B (B, .)/(B, B). Only K (and the fluxes) survive and
  the fixed points satisfy J = lambda B with lambda = (J, B)/(B, B), a Taylor
  (Beltrami) state. This is the global, rank-one analogue of the
  Bressan-Kraus-Morrison-Maj metric that dissipates energy at fixed helicity;
  they report that topology is not preserved [V, their remark after the
  critical condition].

Both are metriplectic in the sense of Morrison's split: the Poisson part is
zero here (no flow inertia), so only the metric part acts. Finite pressure,
flow and the stepped-pressure closure are not part of this stage.

## Discrete FEEC form

Spaces and matrices are those of NN1 (`docs/equilibrium-nonnested.md`): real
Fourier blocks b = 0 (n = 0), 2n-1 (cos), 2n (sin); V1 = Nedelec x Lagrange
(R A_phi), V2 = RT x DG. C is the exact block curl V1 -> V2, M1, M2 the
block-diagonal masses (weights 1, 1/2), P the metric-free V1-V2 pairing, K =
C^T M2 C. A boundary trace (tangential A) is fixed; I is the set of interior
V1 dofs. The harmonic enters through the loads c_K = C^T <psi, h> and
c_H = <phi, h>.

    W(A) = pi (A.K A + 2 A.c_K + <h,h>) / mu0,  K(A) = 2 pi (A.P C A + 2 A.c_H),
    J  = M1^{-1} (K A + c_K)|_I,               B1 = M1^{-1} (P C A + c_H)|_I.

J and B1 are the V1 projections of curl* B and of B (He et al.'s j_h and
H_h). The semi-discrete flow is

    ideal:  M1 dA/dt = < phi_i . (J x B1) x B1 >_Q,
    Taylor: M1 dA/dt = -M1 (J - lambda B1),  lambda = (J, B1)_M / (B1, B1)_M,

with Q the cached meridian quadrature times an nphi-point mean in phi
(nphi = 4N + 1 integrates the cubic product against a degree-N test function
exactly). The identities do not need Q to be exact, only that the load and the
dissipation use the same Q [V, algebra below].

Invariants, all by construction:

1. div B = 0: B = C A with the exact complex, nn_divergence below 1e-11 [V].
2. Normal B on the wall and every flux through a surface bounded on the wall:
   the update lives in I, and C maps zero-trace V1 to zero-normal V2; the
   toroidal flux l^T C A is unchanged because l^T C vanishes on I (discrete
   Stokes). Measured drift 0 exactly [V, tests].
3. Helicity: K(A + d) - K(A) = 4 pi d.(P C (A + d/2) + c_H) = 4 pi d.M1 B1(A + d/2)
   for d in I, using the symmetry of P C on interior columns (integration by
   parts with zero trace; holds to roundoff, measured drift <= 2e-15) [V].
   For the ideal law d.M1 B1 = dt <B1 . (J x B1) x B1>_Q = 0 pointwise at every
   quadrature node; for the Taylor law (J - lambda B1, B1)_M = 0 by the choice
   of lambda [V, FortSym].
4. Energy: W(A + d) - W(A) = (2 pi/mu0) d.M1 J(A + d/2) = -(2 pi dt/mu0)
   <|J x B1|^2>_Q (ideal) or -(2 pi dt/mu0) |J - lambda B1|^2_M (Taylor), with
   J, B1, lambda at the midpoint [V, FortSym and tests].

Items 3 and 4 hold exactly for the implicit midpoint rule because K and W are
quadratic in A (Gauss collocation conserves quadratic invariants; He et al.
Theorem 2 argues the same for their scheme [V]).

## Time step and solver

Implicit midpoint: M1 d = dt Phi(A + d/2). With B1 (ideal) or lambda (Taylor)
frozen the system is linear,

    (M1 - dt/2 L) d = dt Phi(A),   L x = <phi . (J(x) x B1) x B1>  or  -(K - lambda P C) x,

and is solved by FortNum FGMRES, right-preconditioned by a FortSparse factor
of M1 + (dt/2) beta K (beta = <|B1|^2>/V for the ideal law, 1 for Taylor). The
ideal law runs Picard on B1 to a relative change of 1e-10. The Taylor law runs
a secant iteration on lambda for the helicity defect h(lambda) = d.M1 B1(A + d/2),
whose zero is exactly the midpoint solution. A final scalar step d += s B1,
with s the smaller root of the quadratic K(A + d + s B1) = K(A), removes the
remaining defect to roundoff; its relative size is reported (<= 1e-6 ideal,
<= 1e-6 Taylor in the tests). MRX restores helicity with the same quadratic
root (arXiv:2510.26986 v3, eq. 62 [V]).

Implicit midpoint is A-stable but not L-stable: a mode with rate r is
multiplied by (1 - r dt/2)/(1 + r dt/2), which is close to -1 for r dt >> 2.
Growing dt without bound therefore stalls the force (observed: 0.69 -> 0.16
after 300 steps with dt -> 1e6). The step therefore cycles geometrically from
dt to dt_max (factor 1.5); within each cycle every rate between 2/dt_max and
2/dt is damped by at least a factor 0.1, and every step keeps the invariants.
The Taylor oracle then reaches the hand-off force 1e-5 in 31 steps [V].
Rejected steps (energy increase beyond 1e-13 W, failed Picard or FGMRES)
quarter dt.

The pointwise metric is the FortSym leaf `friction_drift` (with JVP and VJP).
The quadrature loop is OpenMP parallel over triangles with cached physical
basis values and a colour-free serial scatter of per-triangle loads.

## Hand-off to Newton

The relaxation stops when a dimensionless force falls below `handoff`:

* ideal: |J x B1|_Q sqrt(V) L / (|B1|_Q^2), the force relative to B^2/L;
* Taylor: |J - lambda B1|_M L / |B1|_M.

Newton then solves the same discrete equation: the Taylor state is passed to
`beltrami_constraint('helicity', K)` with the bracket lambda +/- 1e-3 (the
relaxed multiplier is the initial guess and the bracket width), the vacuum
state to the NN1 `nn_solve` / `nn_vacuum_mode` Newton steps, each with its own
1e-9 residual gate. The hand-off threshold is a globalisation choice, not an
accuracy claim; accuracy is owned by the Newton gate.

## Symbolic verification

`gen_metriplectic` proves with the FortSym native zero test (and aborts on
failure): the helicity Casimir b.((j x b) x b) = 0; the dissipation
j.((j x b) x b) = -|j x b|^2; metric symmetry; the projector form
(j x b) x b = -(|b|^2 I - b b^T) j (three components); the Taylor projector
annihilates B1 and its dissipation equals |J - lambda B1|^2; and the
implicit-midpoint identity K(a+d) - K(a) = 2 d.(H(a + d/2) + c) for a symmetric
3x3 H. A negative control (j.((j x b) x b) is not zero) guards against a
vacuous engine. The CTest `metriplectic_identities_and_regeneration` reruns
the generator and compares the checked-in leaf byte for byte [V].

## Limits

* Topology preservation is exact only in the continuum. The discrete scheme
  keeps helicity and fluxes to roundoff, which is a global barrier (Arnold
  inequality) but not the conservation of every field line; grid-scale
  reconnection remains possible (MRX §6.1, He et al. §4 [V]). The island test
  measures the outcome for one case; it is not a topology certificate.
* The ideal linear solves use a scalar-weighted curl-curl preconditioner,
  which ignores the field-aligned null space of D(B1); FGMRES iterations grow
  with dt. Auxiliary-space (Hiptmair-Xu) preconditioning of the H(curl) block is
  the planned remedy (decision 85a); no speed claim is made.
* The stage acts on the NN1 complex (cylindrical or periodic-cylinder chart,
  identity metric). The mapped (phi-dependent) metric, finite pressure and the
  nested-map free-boundary lane are not covered. The FB3D N=2 stall globalises
  on a residual merit because its Lagrangian is a saddle (vacuum co-energy,
  fixed-current vertical mode); a relaxation there must act on the reduced
  energy after eliminating the vacuum potential [H, design not implemented].
* The helical Chandrasekhar-Kendall branch (m=1, mu a > 3.11) is not tested.
